Biomes:

Distinct geographical ecosystems, known as biomes, can help broadly estimate the location.

Grasslands:
Split into sub-categories of tropical and temperate, grasslands are characterized by flat land and grass. Well known grasslands include the Pampas (South America), Veldt (South Africa), Steppe (Eurasia), and Great Plains (US).

Rainforests:
Like grasslands, they are also split into tropical rainforests and temperate rainforests. They are characterized by high humidity, layers of vegetation, and (sometimes) tropical plants.

Deserts:
Dry, arid, sandy. Plant life is naturally scarce and mainly consists of cacti and shrubs. Well known deserts include the Sahara, Arabian, and Chihuahuan Deserts.

Temperate Forest:
Local deciduous (vegetation that shed and grow their leaves throughout the four seasons) plants along with conifers. Prominent in Central/Western Europe, Eastern North America, and Northern Asia.

Agriculture:

Biomes are great, but can't be efficiently applied to less remote areas. For example, farmland can easily alter the vegetation and throw off those locating solely based on biomes. This is why it's also important to get familiar with agriculture. The most common crops in the world are sugarcane, corn, rice, and wheat. These, along with some other ones, are discussed below!

Sugarcane, with 1.98 billion metric tons produced worldwide, is first in crop production. Its characterized by it's stalk-like structure and long, narrow leaves. After harvesting, it is made into biofuel, sugar, and molasses. Places with prominent sugarcane fields include São Paulo, Brazil; Queensland, Australia; the Philippines; and Java, Indonesia.

Also known as Maize, corn is second in the world in terms of crop production. Corn grows all over the world (except for Antarctica), with United States and China growing more than 50% of the total crop yield.

Rice is usually grown in flooded fields such as terraces or paddies. This makes rice fields relatively easy to identify. Rice is grown all over Asia, with the top five countries being China, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, and Vietnam.

Cassava is used to make tapioca starch, which is then used to make bubble tea pearls! They have a very distinct small, digitate leaf and grows in tropical and subtropical areas.


Resources:

An important part of geolocation with plants is identifying the plants themselves. Get familiar with the types of plants in each region or biome, and work from there! iNaturalist is a pretty reliable and accurate plant identifier for geolocation.

After identifying the crop, use the USDA website to find more information on where it grows! It has some nice resources about agriculture distribution and maps of crop density in each country.

Misc Metas:

Eucalyptus trees often grow on Australian roadsides. These trees have a distinct bark that sheds and leaves a white, smooth underbark

Terraces are stair-shaped farmland that helps with irrigation and prevents erosion. They are mainly located in East and Southeasy Asia and Peru (It's more of a historical thing in Peru).

Lots of trees in Ukraine (and some other Eastern European countries) have their tree trunks painted white. This is done sometimes for pest avoidance and other times as part of a tradition.
